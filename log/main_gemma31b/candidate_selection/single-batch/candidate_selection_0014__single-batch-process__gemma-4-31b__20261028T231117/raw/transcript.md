## Round 2
### Talk
#### Turn 1 of 4
- **Benjamin**: It seems our history is bleak. The eastern avenue trees died due to poor aftercare, though the overflow basin is still solid.
- **Misaki**: The ground is a risk. Firms are flagging clay subsoil and slippage. We should avoid heavy earth-moving if possible.
- **Mio**: The funding is restrictive! The habitat fund only pays for planting and water, not buildings. Plus, greenery costs triple to maintain.
- **Asuka**: Our staff is depleted. We've gone from eighteen posts to seven. We are relying heavily on bought-in services for specialist care.
- **Minako**: People are using the park as a thoroughfare to the tram, sticking to firm ground. Also, drainage issues are doubling.
#### Turn 2 of 4
- **Benjamin**: @Misaki, if the soil is that unstable, any plan requiring massive excavation is a recipe for disaster.
- **Misaki**: Precisely. Shallow work is fine, but deep digging will lead to the "drifting" dates I fear.
- **Mio**: @Asuka, if we have so few staff, we absolutely cannot afford a high-maintenance landscape. The ledger proves it's too expensive.
- **Asuka**: I agree. However, we do have volunteer groups for habitat and sports. That's a potential resource we can leverage.
- **Minako**: @Mio, the heat readings were high last August. We need shade or water, but it must be something that doesn't clog the drains.
#### Turn 3 of 4
- **Benjamin**: The fact that the overflow basin survived while the pavement failed suggests that "natural" water management is more durable here.
- **Misaki**: But we must be careful. If we go too "natural" with heavy planting, we hit that clay slippage risk.
- **Mio**: And the habitat fund only pays for that specific type of work. We can't afford to build a plaza if the fund won't cover it.
- **Asuka**: We have to balance the fund's requirements with the fact that we have almost no one to mow a lawn or prune trees.
- **Minako**: If people are just crossing the site to the tram, we shouldn't put obstacles in their way.
#### Turn 4 of 4
- **Benjamin**: It sounds like we need something durable, low-excavation, and potentially funded by the habitat grant.
- **Misaki**: And something that doesn't require the whole site to be closed at once, given the depot notes.
- **Mio**: Low maintenance is non-negotiable. I won't support a plan that bankrupts the upkeep budget.
- **Asuka**: Let's see the candidates and see which one fits these harsh realities.
- **Minako**: Agreed. Let's look at the options.
## Round 3
### Talk
#### Turn 1 of 4
- **Benjamin**: We have Market Plaza, Woodland Walk, Wetland Restoration, and Sports Terraces. The Plaza seems too "hard" given the previous pavement failures.
- **Misaki**: The Woodland Walk might be too much earth-moving for the clay. Sports Terraces also sound like a construction nightmare.
- **Mio**: The Wetland Restoration fits the habitat fund perfectly. It covers planting and water management, which is all the fund pays for.
- **Asuka**: Wetland care is specialist, but we have volunteers who already work on habitat tasks upriver. They could help.
- **Minako**: Wetland Restoration also addresses the drainage callouts and the August heat. It works with the water, not against it.
#### Turn 2 of 4
- **Benjamin**: @Minako, does it interfere with the tram commuters? Boardwalks could keep people on firm ground while preserving the margins.
- **Misaki**: It's likely shallow work compared to building courts or a massive plaza. It's the safest bet for the soil.
- **Mio**: And it avoids the high cost of hard-surfacing maintenance that I saw in the ledgers.
- **Asuka**: The Sports Terraces would require too much stewarding. We don't have the staff for that, even with the casual pool.
- **Minako**: The Market Plaza would just be a heat sink in August. The wetlands provide actual cooling.
#### Turn 3 of 4

- **Asuka**: With the habitat volunteers already in place, it's the most sustainable option for our limited staff.
- **Minako**: It solves the drainage problem and respects the way the land behaves. I vote for Wetland Restoration.
