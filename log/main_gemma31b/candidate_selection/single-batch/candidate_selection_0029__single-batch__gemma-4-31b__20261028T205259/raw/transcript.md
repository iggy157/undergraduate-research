## Round 2
### Talk
#### Turn 1 of 4
- **Shizue**: It seems our lab is severely understaffed, and our volunteers are mostly from the history circle. We must be careful with the workload.
- **Shion**: We've had several openings slip due to paperwork delays, and powered displays are prone to breaking down during gallery hours.
- **May**: People love it when there's a human presence or a mechanism moving. Static cases just don't bring them back for a second visit.
- **Misaki**: The suspended bay has a strict weight limit, and our evening hires mean we can't have fragile, open arrangements that can't be moved.
- **Sakura**: Transport costs are huge—nearly a third of installation! Also, bespoke mounts are getting very expensive. We need things that come with their own supports.
#### Turn 2 of 4
- **Shizue**: @Sakura, that is a worrying cost. Perhaps we should look for something that doesn't require so many expensive loans.
- **Shion**: @May, if mechanisms attract crowds but break often, we are trading popularity for a lot of stress and roped-off areas.
- **May**: @Shion, but the data shows that's what the visitors want. We just need a reliable way to do it.
- **Misaki**: @Shizue, if the lab is backed up, we certainly cannot afford an exhibition that requires heavy conservation work on-site.
- **Sakura**: @Misaki, does that mean we should avoid heavy things? I don't want the floor to break!
#### Turn 3 of 4

- **Shion**: We should avoid complex overseas loans with tight cut-offs and high-maintenance electronics if we want a smooth season.

- **Misaki**: Exactly. And it must be light enough for the bay and flexible enough for the evening caterers to pass through.
- **Sakura**: So, no heavy stones and no fancy expensive mounts! Let's find something simple but fun.
