## Round 2
### Talk
#### Turn 1 of 4
- **Shizue**: It seems some roads close in winter, but community halls stay open. Also, the grants only cover session fees for a year.
- **Jonathan**: The outer wards have lost bus links, making the trip an hour. Meanwhile, our rooms are full, but people use the catalogue late at night.
- **Rin**: I found out! Outreach used to be super popular in outer wards, and some old evening groups are still going without any help!
- **Benjamin**: The vehicle lines overran significantly last year. Also, the grant structure means we face a funding cliff after twelve months for operations.
- **Minako**: We have retired tradespeople who can help with repairs, but no one on the payroll is trained to supervise outside facilitators.
#### Turn 2 of 4
- **Shizue**: @Benjamin, if the vehicle costs are so high and the grants are short, a mobile service might be very risky.
- **Jonathan**: But @Rin's discovery about the outer wards is crucial. People can't get to us, and they used to love the outreach.
- **Rin**: See! We should do something like those old groups that didn't need us to hold their hands.
- **Benjamin**: A mobile service is a financial trap if the running costs are volatile and the grant is temporary. We need something more stable.
- **Minako**: And we lack the supervisory staff for complex new facilitator-led programs. We should lean on existing community skills.
#### Turn 3 of 4

- **Benjamin**: Precisely. We need a model where the capital investment is handled by the endowment, not the fragile operating budget.
- **Minako**: The tool certification is also a problem; our expert retired. A workshop would need a new way to stay safe.

## Round 3
### Talk
#### Turn 1 of 4
- **Shizue**: We have four choices: a Mobile Branch, a Maker Workshop, Language Circles, or a Digital Archive.
- **Jonathan**: The Mobile Branch solves the bus issue, but @Benjamin mentioned the costs are unstable. Language Circles could work if they are partner-run.
- **Rin**: The Digital Archive sounds like the failed project from six years ago. I don't like it!
- **Benjamin**: The Maker Workshop uses the endowment for tools, which is stable. Language Circles rely on session fees that expire.
- **Minako**: The Workshop is risky because we have no certified technician. Language Circles need supervisors we don't have.
#### Turn 2 of 4
- **Shizue**: The Mobile Branch is vulnerable to those winter road closures I found. It's not a reliable year-round promise.
- **Jonathan**: If the Mobile Branch is out, Language Circles in community halls are the best way to reach the outer wards.
- **Rin**: But the old evening groups worked because they were independent! Language Circles fit that pattern.
- **Benjamin**: From a budget perspective, the Maker Workshop is the cleanest, but the staffing gap Minako mentioned is a logical hurdle.
- **Minako**: I agree. The Language Circles, if coordinated with partner organizations, shift the supervisory burden away from our staff.
#### Turn 3 of 4
- **Shizue**: I feel the Language Circles are the gentlest and most sustainable way to help our neighbors.
- **Jonathan**: They solve the accessibility problem without needing a bus or a van. I support them.
- **Rin**: They are like the successful old groups! I vote for the circles!
- **Benjamin**: While the Workshop is financially attractive, the lack of a technician makes it impractical. Language Circles are a rational compromise.
- **Minako**: Since they use partner organizations, it doesn't overload our current roster. I am convinced.
