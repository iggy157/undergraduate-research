## Round 2
### Talk
#### Turn 1 of 4
- **Mio**: Most of our costs are fixed—hall hire and insurance. Plus, many people struggle to pay the full amount upfront.
- **Jonathan**: It's worse than we thought. Turnout dropped after the summer door charges. People stopped feeling like the evenings belonged to them.
- **Takumi**: Exactly! A tiny group does all the work and attends everything, while others barely show up but pay the same. It's unfair.
- **Toshio**: Chasing small payments is a nightmare. The treasurer spent six hours a month on it. Also, named grades caused friction in the past.
- **Kenji**: Newcomers are scared of the big upfront cost. Many taster guests won't join because they can't commit to a whole year.
#### Turn 2 of 4
- **Mio**: If we have high fixed costs, we can't just charge per event, or we'll go broke if turnout is low.
- **Jonathan**: @Takumi, I understand the unfairness, but if we charge per event, we create a psychological barrier that keeps people away.
- **Takumi**: But why should the regulars subsidize people who never come? We need a system that reflects actual usage.
- **Toshio**: @Takumi, any system with frequent small payments will kill the next treasurer. We need something streamlined.
- **Kenji**: And we can't have "grades" or "levels." It creates a hierarchy that makes the club feel less inclusive.
#### Turn 3 of 4
- **Mio**: We need a balance. We need the money guaranteed, but we can't ignore that some people need payment plans.
- **Jonathan**: If we make it a "pay-as-you-go" system, the regulars will feel the cost every single time they walk through the door.
- **Takumi**: Then maybe a tiered system? High usage for those who come every week, and a low fee for the occasional visitors.
- **Toshio**: @Takumi, the records show that named grades caused social friction. People in the lower tier stopped volunteering.
- **Kenji**: I agree with Toshio. Let's not label people. But we have to find a way to let people join mid-year easily.
#### Turn 4 of 4
- **Mio**: So we have a conflict: fixed costs vs. accessibility, and fairness vs. administrative ease.
- **Jonathan**: The most important thing is that members feel welcome to attend without calculating the cost in their heads.
- **Takumi**: I still think the current "everyone pays the same" is a recipe for resentment among the core members.
- **Toshio**: Whatever we choose, it must not involve the treasurer spending their entire month chasing pennies.
- **Kenji**: Let's look at the options and see which one hurts the fewest people.
