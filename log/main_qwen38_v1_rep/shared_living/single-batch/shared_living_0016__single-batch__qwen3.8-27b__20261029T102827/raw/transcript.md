## Round 2
### Talk
#### Turn 1 of 4
- **Minato**: The treasurer spent six hours a month chasing individual payments.
- **Ryuji**: The ledger shows seventy percent of costs are fixed, like hall hire.
- **Minako**: Nine people who didn't join said they couldn't commit to a whole year.
- **May**: Turnout dropped by a third after we tried charging per event.
- **Shunichi**: The same nine people do the setup and are the top attendees.
#### Turn 2 of 4
- **Minato**: @Ryuji, if costs are fixed, why do we chase individual payments?
- **Ryuji**: Because the bank statements show a third of renewals need reminders.
- **Minako**: The quiet members are uneasy about what their two visits cost.
- **May**: People stopped treating the evenings as theirs when we charged per door.
- **Shunichi**: The sign-in sheets show a quarter of members do two-thirds of the work.
#### Turn 3 of 4
- **Minato**: Chasing money is too much work. We need a simpler way.
- **Ryuji**: Fixed costs mean we need reliable income. A flat fee might work.
- **Minako**: If we make it too visible, people will feel judged.
- **May**: Activity-based charging made people feel like guests, not members.
- **Shunichi**: If we charge per event, the heavy users pay more, but the light users might leave.
#### Turn 4 of 4
- **Minato**: I am leaning towards a flat fee to reduce the admin burden.
- **Ryuji**: A flat fee covers the fixed costs without constant chasing.
- **Minako**: But will it feel fair to those who come rarely?
- **May**: It feels safer. People know what they owe at the start.
- **Shunichi**: It is the simplest. Let us see if the others agree.
