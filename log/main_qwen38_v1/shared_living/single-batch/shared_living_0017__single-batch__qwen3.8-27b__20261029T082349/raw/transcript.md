## Round 2
### Talk
#### Turn 1 of 4
- **Asuka**: I watched the mornings. Eleven bikes leave between seven and nine. Four times, someone had to shift another machine.
- **Ryuji**: The ledger shows little capital work. The ward park costs 2,600 yen monthly, with fourteen households waiting.
- **George**: The minutes show the same flats held covered spots for years. The sticker register shows varying bike counts.
- **Shunichi**: The complaint log has nine notes about leaning bikes. The neighbouring block had arrears and absenteeism.
- **Mio**: The treasurer spends three hours a month on cash. The display frame takes twenty seconds to load and needs greasing.

#### Turn 2 of 4
- **Asuka**: @Ryuji, if the ward park is full, we cannot just send people there. We need internal order.

- **George**: @Shunichi, the neighbouring block's failure suggests that charging can alienate people.
- **Shunichi**: @George, yes. But the complaint log shows that doing nothing also fails. We need a middle ground.
- **Mio**: @Ryuji, three hours a month is significant. Adding billing would double that. Who has time?

#### Turn 3 of 4
- **Asuka**: The rain exposure is bad past the downpipe. We need to prioritize dry spots for those who need them.
- **Ryuji**: The ledger is flat. Any new cost must be covered by a levy or income.
- **George**: The sticker register shows some flats have three bikes. Equal claim is not equal need.
- **Shunichi**: If we make it a lottery, it feels random. If we make it paid, it feels exclusionary.
- **Mio**: The display frame is physically demanding. Shared racks might be easier to maintain than individual billing.

#### Turn 4 of 4

- **Ryuji**: I am concerned about the administrative cost of any complex system.
- **George**: I want to ensure the process is transparent and not just a repeat of the past.
- **Shunichi**: I want to avoid a system that makes people hide their bikes or avoid the lobby.
- **Mio**: I want a system that does not fall on one person's shoulders.
