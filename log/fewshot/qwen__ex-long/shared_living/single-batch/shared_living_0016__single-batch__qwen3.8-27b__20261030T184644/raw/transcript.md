## Round 2
### Talk
#### Turn 1 of 4
- **Minato**: The treasurer logged six hours a month chasing individual payments. That is unsustainable.
- **Ryuji**: The ledger shows seventy percent of costs are fixed, like hall hire. We pay whether we come or not.
- **Minako**: The sign-in sheets show a quarter of members do two-thirds of the work. That is a huge gap.
- **May**: Since the summer outings had a charge, weekday turnout dropped by a third. People stopped feeling it was theirs.
- **Shunichi**: The rota confirms it. The same nine people set up and lock up. They are the most active.

- **Minako**: @Shunichi, that imbalance is dangerous. If the active members feel exploited, they will leave.
- **May**: @Minako, I agree. The winter social notes say quiet members feel uneasy about the cost per visit.
- **Shunichi**: @May, unease is an understatement. It’s being said openly now. We need a fairer system.
#### Turn 3 of 4
- **Minato**: @Ryuji, a flat fee might be the only way to stop the chasing. One payment, done.
- **Ryuji**: @Minato, a flat fee is risky if costs rise. But activity-based charging creates more admin.
- **Minako**: @Ryuji, tiered membership could work. Let people choose their level of involvement.
- **May**: @Minako, tiers might feel exclusive. I worry it will make the quiet members feel like outsiders.
- **Shunichi**: @May, tiers are better than nothing. At least it acknowledges that not everyone does the same.
#### Turn 4 of 4
- **Minato**: I prefer the flat fee. It’s simple and stops the endless chasing.
- **Ryuji**: I am cautious. A flat fee is predictable, but we must ensure it covers the fixed costs.
- **Minako**: I lean towards tiers. It gives people a choice and respects their different levels of participation.
- **May**: I am worried about tiers. I think a flat fee is safer for keeping everyone included.
- **Shunichi**: I support tiers. It’s the only way to address the imbalance without punishing the active.
