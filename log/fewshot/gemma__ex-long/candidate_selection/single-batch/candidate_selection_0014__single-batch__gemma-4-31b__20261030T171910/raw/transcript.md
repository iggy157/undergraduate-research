## Round 2
### Talk
#### Turn 1 of 4
- **Benjamin**: My findings are sobering. While our water-based infrastructure has held up, our surfacing is a disaster, and our tree planting failed miserably due to poor aftercare.
- **Misaki**: The ground is a risk. Firms are warning about clay subsoil and slippage, though small-scale work is fine. Also, we can't hoard the center or gate without closing the park.
- **Mio**: Oh wow! I found out that planting and water management are way more expensive to maintain than hard surfaces. Plus, the habitat fund only pays for the green stuff!
- **Asuka**: Our internal staff is depleted. We've lost over half our groundskeepers. We rely on contractors for specialist care, though we have some volunteers for habitats and sports.
- **Minako**: The site is under pressure. People mostly use the firm ground to reach the tram, and our drainage is failing, while the heat in the open areas is becoming extreme.
#### Turn 2 of 4
- **Benjamin**: @Misaki, if the center is a bottleneck for construction, we should avoid any plan that requires massive central excavation.
- **Misaki**: @Benjamin, I agree. The clay subsoil makes any deep digging a gamble. Shallow work is safer and more predictable.
- **Mio**: @Asuka, if we don't have enough staff, maybe we should pick something that doesn't need a ton of gardening? The upkeep costs for plants are scary!
- **Asuka**: @Mio, that is a logical point. We have volunteers for specific tasks, but the general roll is too short to handle a high-maintenance landscape.
- **Minako**: @Benjamin, the drainage issues and heat readings suggest that simply paving everything over might worsen the environment, even if it's easier to maintain.
#### Turn 3 of 4
- **Benjamin**: @Minako, a fair point. However, the record shows that our "green" attempts often die from neglect. We need a balance of utility and survival.
- **Misaki**: @Minako, the weekday crossing data is key. People want a direct, firm path to the tram. Any plan that forces them into mud or reeds will fail.
- **Mio**: But the habitat fund is so much money! It would be a shame to ignore it, as long as we can afford to keep it later.
- **Asuka**: @Mio, the fund pays for the build, but the city pays for the upkeep. With only seven groundskeepers left, we cannot afford a "beautiful" mistake.
- **Minako**: Exactly. We need something that handles the water, provides shade for the heat, but respects the flow of the commuters.
#### Turn 4 of 4
- **Benjamin**: It seems we are searching for a solution that is structurally shallow, operationally lean, and environmentally functional.
- **Misaki**: And one that doesn't require us to shut down the main gate for months.
- **Mio**: I just hope we don't pick something that bankrupts the maintenance budget in three years!
- **Asuka**: I agree. The human capacity to maintain the park is our tightest constraint.
- **Minako**: Over
## Round 3
### Talk
#### Turn 1 of 4
- **Benjamin**: The candidates are Market Plaza, Woodland Walk, Wetland Restoration, and Sports Terraces. Given the failure of previous plantings, Woodland Walk seems a risky gamble.
- **Misaki**: Market Plaza and Sports Terraces involve surfacing. Wetland Restoration requires significant earth-moving and drainage changes, which the firms warned about.
- **Mio**: Market Plaza is hard-surfaced, which is cheaper to maintain! But Wetland Restoration fits the habitat fund perfectly. I'm torn.
- **Asuka**: Sports Terraces would utilize the youth league volunteers. Market Plaza is lean. Woodland Walk and Wetland Restoration require specialist care we no longer have.
- **Minako**: Market Plaza addresses the heat with shade and the tram commuters with firm ground. Wetland Restoration addresses the drainage but might obstruct the crossings.
#### Turn 2 of 4
- **Benjamin**: @Asuka, the Sports Terraces' surfacing would likely end up patched and broken like the civic hall apron. Market Plaza is more flexible.
- **Misaki**: @Minako, Market Plaza is the safest for the ground. It's shallow work. Wetland Restoration is a nightmare for clay subsoil and slippage.
- **Mio**: @Benjamin, if we go with Market Plaza, we lose the habitat fund money. But if we go with Wetland, the upkeep costs will be triple!
- **Asuka**: @Mio, the upkeep is the priority. Market Plaza is the most sustainable for our current staffing levels.
- **Minako**: I agree. Market Plaza provides the shade we need for the August heat and keeps the tram path clear.
#### Turn 3 of 4
- **Benjamin**: The logic is consistent. Market Plaza avoids the biological volatility of the Woodland and the structural risks of the Wetland.
- **Misaki**: It also avoids the hoarding issues of the center if we phase the stall infrastructure correctly.
- **Mio**: I guess the long-term budget is more important than the initial grant. I'm starting to lean toward the Plaza.
- **Asuka**: It is the only choice that doesn't assume we have a workforce that doesn't exist.
- **Minako**: It is the most pragmatic response to the actual behavior of the people and the land.
#### Turn 4 of 4
- **Benjamin**: I am satisfied. Market Plaza is the most rational choice. I vote for Market Plaza.
- **Misaki**: I agree. It minimizes the risk of slippage and delivery delays. I vote for Market Plaza.
- **Mio**: Okay! Let's save the maintenance budget. I vote for Market Plaza!
- **Asuka**: For the sake of the staff and the rota, I vote for Market Plaza.
- **Minako**: It fits the usage patterns and the climate needs. I vote for Market Plaza.
