## Round 2
### Talk
#### Turn 1 of 4
- **Minato**: It's sad, but the coordination group is understaffed and our developers are busy until the third quarter. We can't ask them for more.
- **Jonathan**: I found that network coverage at customer plants is very unreliable, and some visitor approvals take weeks. Also, tools requiring daily entry from everyone usually fail.
- **George**: The capital budget is frozen, though we have some software allowance. Interestingly, fixed-fee work is stable, but per-head licences will get more expensive as we grow.
- **May**: Jonathan is right about the rollouts. Also, most cross-unit standardization efforts failed because they had no owner, except for the tool room walkthroughs.
- **Midori**: People are losing an hour every day just copying records. Also, we have misrouted deliveries because different units have different addresses for the same firms.
#### Turn 2 of 4
- **Minato**: @George, if the budget is frozen, does that mean we can only do things that use the software allowance?
- **Jonathan**: @Midori, the copying and delivery issues sound like they could be solved by a central system, but we must avoid anything that requires constant daily data entry.
- **George**: @Minato, essentially yes. We must be very careful about recurring costs, especially if they scale with the number of users.
- **May**: @Jonathan, the fact that "narrow tools" survive while broad ones fail is a huge warning. We shouldn't pick something that tries to force everyone to change their habits.
- **Midori**: @May, I agree. But the waste of time is real. If we can fix the address and copying issues without adding a huge burden, it would help everyone.
#### Turn 3 of 4
- **Minato**: @Jonathan, if the network is bad at plants, maybe we shouldn't pick something that needs a constant internet connection?
- **Jonathan**: @Minato, that is a very sharp observation. Any field-based tool would be a gamble given the signal drops and the approval delays.
- **George**: I am concerned that any project requiring a large-scale rollout of licences will eventually break our budget in the renewal year.
- **May**: It seems we need something narrow, low-maintenance, and not dependent on external site access or a massive amount of new developer time.
- **Midori**: I just want something that stops the frustration of the staff. If we can consolidate the records, the daily stress would go down.

## Round 3
### Talk
#### Turn 1 of 4
- **Minato**: The candidates are Workflow Assistant, Training Studio, Supplier Data Hub, and Field Service App. I think the Workflow Assistant sounds like it might be too much extra work.
- **Jonathan**: I must object to the Field Service App. With the network issues and visitor approvals I found, it is far too risky to deploy.
- **George**: The Supplier Data Hub sounds like it could involve many licences. The Training Studio might be a fixed-fee production, which is safer for the budget.
- **May**: The Supplier Data Hub is a "standardization effort," and those usually fail without an owner. The Workflow Assistant requires daily entry, which also fails.
- **Midori**: The Supplier Data Hub would solve the misrouted deliveries and the copying of records. It addresses the actual problems the staff are facing.
#### Turn 2 of 4
- **Minato**: @Midori, but who will update the Hub? If the coordination group is already empty, will it just be more work for the same tired people?
- **Jonathan**: @Minato, that's a fair point. The Training Studio is a set of modules; once they are built, they don't require daily maintenance from the staff.
- **George**: @Midori, if the Hub uses per-head licences for every unit, it will be a financial burden. The Training Studio is a one-time production cost.
- **May**: @George, I agree. The Training Studio is a "narrow tool" for skill transfer, similar to the tool room walkthroughs that actually worked.
- **Midori**: @May, but the Training Studio doesn't stop the delivery errors or the copying. The Hub is more useful, even if it's harder to start.
#### Turn 3 of 4
- **Minato**: I feel the Training Studio is the kindest choice. It helps people learn without adding a new daily chore to their list.
- **Jonathan**: I agree. It avoids the network risks of the App and the "daily entry" failure pattern of the Assistant and the Hub.
- **George**: From a budget perspective, the Training Studio is the most predictable. No scaling licences, no frozen capital issues.
- **May**: It's the only one that fits the pattern of success. It's a resource, not a process. I vote for the Training Studio.
- **Midori**: I still wish we could fix the data issues, but I don't want to pick something that will be dropped after a year. I'll go with the Studio.
#### Turn 4 of 4
- **Minato**: I vote for the Training Studio.
- **Jonathan**: I vote for the Training Studio.
- **George**: I vote for the Training Studio.
- **May**: I vote for the Training Studio.
- **Midori**: I vote for the Training Studio.
