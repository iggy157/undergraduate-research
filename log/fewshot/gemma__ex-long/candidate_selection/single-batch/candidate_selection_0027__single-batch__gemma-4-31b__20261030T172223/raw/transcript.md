## Round 3
### Talk
#### Turn 1 of 4
- **Victoria**: Looking at the candidates, the Night Service Extension is tempting because of the pilot, but the roster books show we have no one left to work.
- **Takumi**: The Crosstown Bus Corridor risks the same failure as the previous lane-priority stretches. I'm not sure it's a reliable bet.
- **Toshio**: Station Access Upgrades are too dangerous. The contingency pricing for pre-war shells suggests we'll hit voids and stall for weeks.
- **Daisuke**: The Integrated Fare Platform seems expensive, and since the technology budget is trimmed and ticketing fees are high, I'm hesitant.
- **Sakura**: If the problem is waiting for connections and mid-city slowness, the Bus Corridor might help, but the Fare Platform doesn't fix the time loss.
#### Turn 2 of 4
- **Victoria**: @Sakura, the Bus Corridor requires roadway crews, and they are a third below establishment. We can't build what we can't maintain.
- **Takumi**: @Victoria, what about the Night Service? If the pilot worked, maybe we can adjust the rosters instead of just adding more people?
- **Toshio**: @Takumi, the Night Service requires maintenance windows. But the late-turn pool is already covering overnight works. It's a recipe for burnout.
- **Daisuke**: I... I think the Fare Platform is the only one that doesn't require paving or construction, which avoids the climbing paving rates.
- **Sakura**: But @Daisuke, the journey audits show that paying isn't where the minutes go! It wouldn't actually help the passengers' travel time.
#### Turn 3 of 4
- **Victoria**: I'm torn. But the maintenance contract for lifts and escalators actually has spare capacity. That makes Station Access Upgrades slightly more feasible.
- **Takumi**: @Victoria, but Toshio found that the pre-war shells are full of surprises. Spare crew capacity doesn't help if the ground is a void.
- **Toshio**: Exactly. The only plan that avoids the physical risks of the shells and the staffing crisis of the night shifts is the Fare Platform.
- **Daisuke**: If the other three are impossible due to staff or ground risks, maybe the Fare Platform is the safest, even if the budget is tight.
- **Sakura**: I still hate that it doesn't fix the crowding or the time, but if the others will just stall or fail, I guess it's the most realistic.
#### Turn 4 of 4
- **Victoria**: I agree. Given the staffing shortages in the late-turn pool and roadway crews, the Fare Platform is the only one we can actually implement.
- **Takumi**: It's not the most exciting, but it avoids the "reverted to mixed traffic" fate of the bus lanes. I'll go with the Fare Platform.
- **Toshio**: It avoids the pre-war shell voids and the joint operator data conflicts are a hurdle, but not a physical impossibility. I vote for D.
- **Daisuke**: I feel safer with a plan that doesn't involve climbing paving rates or construction risks. I vote for the Integrated Fare Platform.
- **Sakura**: I'll vote for the Fare Platform too. It's better than a plan that never gets finished because the crews are too tired!
