## Round 3
### Talk
#### Turn 1 of 4
- **Asuka**: Looking at the candidates, the Integrated Fare Platform seems the least taxing on our depleted rosters, provided the technology is stable.
- **Takumi**: I disagree. The journey audits show ticketing isn't the bottleneck. The Crosstown Bus Corridor addresses the actual transit time delays I found.
- **Benjamin**: The Bus Corridor involves paving, and my data shows paving rates are climbing. The Fare Platform might be cheaper if we can move away from multiple contractors.
- **Servas**: The Night Service Extension aligns with the successful pilot I found. However, @Asuka's concerns about the late-turn pool make it very risky.
- **Jonathan**: Station Access Upgrades are a nightmare given the pre-war shell voids. The Bus Corridor is safer, but the Fare Platform avoids the physical ground entirely.
#### Turn 2 of 4
- **Asuka**: @Takumi, the Bus Corridor requires dedicated lanes and boarding islands. Who builds and maintains them? Our roadway crews are already below establishment.
- **Takumi**: @Asuka, it's a necessary risk to fix the transit times. But if the crews are that low, perhaps the Fare Platform is the only "safe" bet.
- **Benjamin**: The Fare Platform replaces three separate contractors. That could potentially stop the bleed in our capital ledger. It's the most logical financial move.
- **Servas**: If we cannot extend the night service due to staffing, and the stations are too risky, the Fare Platform or the Bus Corridor remain.
- **Jonathan**: @Servas, the Fare Platform requires the ferry and rail offices to agree. My findings show they can't even agree on data terms. It might stall indefinitely.
#### Turn 3 of 4
- **Asuka**: @Jonathan, that is a critical point. If the Fare Platform is a political deadlock, the Bus Corridor is the only one left, despite the crew shortage.
- **Takumi**: If the Fare Platform is stalled by bureaucracy, then the Bus Corridor is the only way to actually improve the passenger's journey time.
- **Benjamin**: I dislike the paving costs, but a project that actually gets built is better than a "lean" one that never starts due to committee arguments.
- **Servas**: The Bus Corridor's lane-priority stretches have failed in the past, but the need for mid-city efficiency is clear. It is a pragmatic compromise.
- **Jonathan**: It avoids the pre-war shells and the inter-agency data wars. It is the most likely to actually move forward.
#### Turn 4 of 4
- **Asuka**: I can support the Bus Corridor if we prioritize the boarding islands over complex roadworks to save our crews. I vote for B.
- **Takumi**: It fixes the actual time loss for the passengers. I vote for B.
- **Benjamin**: Despite the paving rates, it's the most viable path that avoids the technology budget cuts. I vote for B.
- **Servas**: It is a balanced choice that addresses current needs while avoiding the staffing crisis of the night extension. I vote for B.
- **Jonathan**: It is the only plan that doesn't rely on the "unrecorded voids" or the joint committee's cooperation. I vote for B.
